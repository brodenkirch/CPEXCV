Data Availability Statement:

Information on the NASA CPEX-CV 2022 campaign is available via Zawislak et al. (2022) and Nowottnick et al. (2024), the former of which includes a link to the main CPEX-CV campaign webpage. Direct links to the CPEX-CV instrument data, including the APR-3, DAWN, and dropsonde data used in the analysis, are also found through Zawislak et al. (2022). The CPEX-CV instrument data is freely accessible upon registering at the NASA EarthData repository.
 
Information on the NASA CPEX 2017 campaign is available via Chen & Zipser (2017), which includes a link to the main CPEX campaign webpage that houses the CPEX science data repository, from which the processed data used in the analysis is accessed through successive links. Data used in the analysis is accessed from the latest versions of the dropsonde, DAWN, and APR-3 folders with no registration required for access. These folders also include README files for more information about the datasets.

Information on the NASA CPEX-AW 2021 campaign is available via Skofronick-Jackson et al. (2021), which includes a link to the main CPEX-AW campaign webpage. Direct links to the CPEX-AW instrument data, including the dropsonde, DAWN, and APR-3 data used in the analysis, are also found through Skofronick-Jackson et al. (2021). The CPEX-AW instrument data is freely accessible upon registering at the NASA EarthData repository.

Information on the NASA CAMP2Ex 2019 campaign is available via Reid et al. (2019), which includes a link to the main CAMP2Ex campaign webpage. Direct links to the CAMP2Ex instrument data, including the dropsonde and APR-3 data used in the analysis, are also found through Reid et al. (2019). The CAMP2Ex instrument data is freely accessible upon registering at the NASA EarthData repository.

TPW data used in the analysis is publicly available via Cooperative Institute for Meteorological Satellite Studies (2025), as is the IMERG and TIMPS data used for analysis through the Goddard Earth Sciences Data and Information Services Center (Huffman et al., 2019) and Russell (2022), respectively. The Sounding/Hodograph Analysis and Research Program in Python (SHARPpy) open-source meteorological package used to calculate PBL depth and wind shear from CPEX-CV in situ observations is publicly available via Blumberg et al. (2017).
